Research date: September 5, 2026. This is a proposal, not a deployed redesign.

Do not publish email addresses or phone numbers in visible content, page source, structured data, or downloadable contact cards. Start with a strong introduction, brief experience summary, the three-step process, a short Dan biography, and a contact form. Expand service pages and interactive demonstrations later. This supersedes the direct-contact recommendations in the original research below; those competitor observations remain useful historical findings, not the selected implementation.

## Recommended positioning

Make source-code analysis the primary offer. Use “we” for Manheim Consulting and identify Dan Manheim as the lead consultant. Attorneys should immediately understand that they can retain the firm for analysis supporting their existing testifying expert. Keep deposition and trial testimony available as a secondary capability.

The position to communicate is experienced, thorough, dependable analysis when the technical work matters. Show how the work is performed and delivered instead of relying on premium adjectives or promises about case outcomes. The competitor's “gold standard” comment is useful positioning input; it should become a public quotation only with accurate wording, attribution, and permission.

## Proposed homepage copy

Eyebrow: **Software litigation consulting**

Headline: **Source code analysis for complex software litigation.**

Short introduction: **We turn complex software into a clear understanding of the system—and claim charts grounded in documented features.**

Expanded introduction: **Led by Dan Manheim, we examine the system, document its key features, and build a clear picture of how it works. We then map that understanding to claim elements, with citations to the documented features supporting each mapping. Counsel and testifying experts receive a technical foundation they can examine and use.**

### The central story: ingest, digest, synthesize

The owner describes a three-stage process. Make this a prominent homepage section and the basis of the interactive demonstration:

1. **Ingest — capture the details.** We examine the system and document its key elements, building the notes that ground the analysis.
2. **Digest — see the whole system.** We turn those notes into a clear overview of the architecture: the major components, their relationships, and how the system works.
3. **Synthesize — connect the system to the claims.** We map claim elements to documented features, producing claim charts with citations supporting each mapping.

The value is the traceable connection between detailed examination, system understanding, and cited work product. Use “each mapping is supported” rather than promising that every asserted claim element will be found; where support is absent or uncertain, the work should make that clear.

Suggested process heading: **Ingest. Digest. Synthesize.** Supporting line: **A clear path from system details to cited claim charts.**

Demonstration concept: use a small fictional system with notes, a legible architecture diagram, and a short claim-chart excerpt. Selecting a claim element highlights its documented feature, the relevant architecture component, and the note supporting the mapping. Clearly label all example materials as illustrative. This is a demonstration of the consulting method, not an automatic claim-chart generator or a public code-upload service. The architecture view is the one place where optional 3D may help; compare its clarity against a simpler 2D version before committing.

The current conversation concept depicts the three steps as a static illustration; the full interaction described above is a proposed implementation.

Primary action: **Discuss a matter**. Also show direct email, click-to-call, and a copy-email action immediately. Secondary action: **Meet Dan Manheim** or **View experience**.

Experience strip, based on the owner's updated notes:

- 100+ litigation cases
- 15+ years of experience
- Led by Dan Manheim

Suggested supporting statement: **Our work includes analysis of software from Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, and other major technology companies.** Place this under “Systems we have analyzed,” separate from client firms. These companies must not be presented as clients or endorsers merely because their systems were analyzed.

For the draft “strong theories for your expert report,” prefer: **We identify the technical findings, test competing explanations, and develop well-supported theories for your expert's report.** This describes a rigorous process and leaves room for findings that challenge an initial theory.

“Eight-figure disputes” can be supporting context if substantiated and appropriate to disclose, but should not be a headline or imply recoveries attributable to the firm. Avoid inventing response-time guarantees, comparative prices, case outcomes, or an unconditional availability promise.

Rename “Practice Areas” to **Technical expertise**:

- Mobile and web applications, including client–server systems
- Smartphones and mobile platforms
- Smart home and IoT systems
- E-commerce and financial technology
- Data processing and algorithms
- Network protocols and core internet technologies

Present litigation services separately from technology domains. Detailed pages for patents, copyright, trade secrets, reverse engineering, prior art, or claim-chart support should describe only work the firm actually offers.

## Contact design and free providers

The homepage should serve both a referral visitor who already wants Dan's contact information and a search visitor assessing fit. Put direct email and phone in the header/hero and footer. On mobile, a compact contact bar can keep them available without covering content. Include a short form with name, work email, optional firm, and a brief message. Phone can be optional. Offer a contact-card download and a CV or concise qualifications document.

Use a small instruction such as “Please keep your initial message general; we can arrange how to share case materials after the initial discussion.” A public inquiry form need not invite uploads or detailed case evidence. Do not promise privilege or confidentiality that the form itself cannot establish.

| Option | Published free allowance | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Formspree](https://help.formspree.io/articles/account-management/account-limits) | 50 submissions/month; 2 linked notification email addresses; 30 days of history. | Keep it if current delivery works and volume stays comfortably within the allowance. Lowest migration effort. |
| [Web3Forms](https://web3forms.com/pricing) | 250 submissions/month; unlimited forms/domains; hCaptcha integration. | More free capacity. Turnstile, domain restriction, and multiple recipients are listed among paid features. |
| Direct email and phone links | No form-service quota or added form-service charge. | Include regardless of form provider; email links depend on the visitor's mail setup, so also show a copyable address. |

Recommendation: retain Formspree initially unless its delivery or allowance is a problem. Provider choice is less consequential than visible direct contact and reliable submission behavior. Pricing and features were checked on the research date and may change.

No form was submitted during this review. The presence of a configured endpoint does not prove delivery to the correct inbox. Before launch, verify a successful delivery, error handling, spam handling, mobile use, and accessible status feedback. Keep the message intact after an error and offer direct email as a fallback.

The opportunities below are hypotheses based on intent and competitor content. They are not guaranteed ranking improvements or a claim that these competitors outrank Manheim for every query.

Add trade-secret/copyright pages only after confirming scope and having enough distinct useful content. Do not manufacture one near-identical page for each keyword or city. Put the six technology areas on the main expertise section initially, then expand only where there is real experience and substantial material to share.

Three initial article concepts:

1. What attorneys should provide before a source-code review begins.
2. Ingest, digest, synthesize: a synthetic example showing system notes, architecture, and a cited claim-chart mapping.
3. How source-code analysts and testifying experts work together.

Each should explain the work in Dan's own terms, identify the author, use original examples, and answer questions counsel actually asks. This is consistent with Google's emphasis on useful, first-hand content. [Google helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

Technical foundation: descriptive page titles and headings; useful unique descriptions; canonical URLs; crawlable internal links; XML sitemap; intentional indexing settings; responsive pages; appropriate Person/Organization structured data matching visible facts; social sharing metadata. Structured data is not a guaranteed rich result or ranking boost. The existing meta-keywords tag does not help Google ranking. [Google SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

Preserve the existing domain and useful URL behavior. A static multipage implementation fits the current GitHub Pages setup. A switch to a JavaScript-heavy application is not required. Keep substantive content available in initial HTML and make the demonstration an enhancement. [Google JavaScript SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics).

Set performance acceptance targets around Google's good Core Web Vitals: LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP below 200 ms, and CLS below 0.1. These are targets, not measurements of the current site. Validate on mobile and monitor real-user results when traffic is sufficient. [Google Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals).

Measure branded versus nonbranded impressions and clicks, landing pages, contact clicks, successful form submissions, and qualified inquiries. Track which inquiries concern source-code analysis versus testimony. Record the baseline before launch and compare over subsequent months. Update accurate professional profiles and seek legitimate links from relevant publications, speaking engagements, or professional associations; do not buy bulk directory links.

## Current-site findings

The live homepage was visually inspected on desktop and its source files reviewed. A full mobile/device audit and a performance benchmark were not performed.

- `index.html:90` — The main heading is the company name and the full-height hero consumes most of the first screen. Lead with the service and bring contact and experience into this space.
- `index.html:126` — Mixed logo backgrounds, internal padding, and visual weights are apparent on the live page. Uniform outer cards do not make the logos optically consistent.
- `index.html:209` — No direct email or phone links in the current homepage, and the form has no native POST action/method. A JavaScript failure can leave submission using default GET behavior to the current page.
- `src/main.js:85` — The script actively removes form action and method attributes. Use progressive enhancement so the form has a dependable native submission route.
- `src/main.js:176` — Form field contents are written to the browser console. Remove production logging of inquiry text and personal details.
- `index.html:57` — Mobile menu button lacks an accessible name and expanded-state attributes; audit keyboard/focus behavior during the redesign.
- `src/main.js:212` — Submission feedback is a temporary toast without an explicit live region. Show accessible inline success/errors near the form.
- `index.html:23` — A JavaScript library URL is loaded as a stylesheet. Remove or correct this unused dependency.
- `index.html:292` — Analytics uses a Universal Analytics ID. Standard UA stopped processing data in 2023; replace with GA4 or another suitable measurement setup. [Google notice](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10089681?hl=en).
- The repository lacks a sitemap, canonical tag, social metadata, and structured data in the main page. Add these as appropriate; absence alone is not proof of an indexing failure.

There is an untracked `new-index.html` and existing user changes. It contains a different contact approach and an address not verified in this review. It was not treated as the live site or as a source of confirmed contact details.
